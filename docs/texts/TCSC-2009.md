# A Simple Time Domain Algorithm for the Detection of Ventricular Fibrillation in Electrocardiogram

**Authors:** Muhammad Abdullah Arafat, Abdul Wadud Chowdhury, Md. Kamrul Hasan
**Journal:** SIViP (Signal, Image and Video Processing), 2011, 5:1–10
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11760-009-0136-1
**Received:** 16 May 2009; **Accepted:** 9 September 2009

---

## Abstract

A new time domain algorithm called **Threshold Crossing Sample Count (TCSC)** is proposed, which is an improved version of TCI (Threshold Crossing Interval). Based on the random behavior of the electrical heart vector in VF. Two simple operations — comparison and count — calculate an effective measure separating life-threatening VF from other rhythms. Tested on complete MIT-BIH arrhythmia and CU databases (>190,000 decisions). TCSC yields the highest AUC among all compared algorithms.

**Keywords:** Ventricular fibrillation, Normal sinus rhythm, Isoelectric level, Threshold crossing

---

## Introduction

VF requires quick accurate detection to trigger defibrillation. Misinterpreting NSR as VF can also be fatal. Most existing methods show poor performance on large diverse databases. TCSC improves on TCI by addressing its known limitations.

---

## TCI Algorithm (Predecessor) — Limitations

The TCI algorithm (Thakor et al. 1990) works on 1-second segments with threshold at 20% of peak amplitude:
$$\text{TCI} = \frac{1000}{(N-1) + \frac{t_2}{t_1+t_2} + \frac{t_3}{t_3+t_4}}$$

**Known limitations:**
1. 1-second window too short — misses R-peaks at low heart rates (<60 bpm)
2. Only positive threshold — fails on leads with negative QRS peaks (e.g., V1)
3. VF may oscillate below threshold — information lost
4. Inter-beat samples crossing threshold counted as pulses — lowers TCI erroneously

## TCSC Algorithm

### Key Insight

In NSR, ECG spends majority of time near the isoelectric line (P-R segments, S-T segments, inter-beat intervals constitute >50% of signal). In VF, irregular waveform keeps samples away from baseline. TCSC counts the **percentage of time** the ECG remains outside a normalized threshold.

### Algorithm Steps

1. **Preprocessing** (using `filtering.m` from Amann et al. [COMP55-2005]):
   - Subtract mean
   - Moving average filter (order 5) — removes high-frequency noise
   - High-pass filter (cutoff 1 Hz) — removes baseline drift
   - Low-pass Butterworth (cutoff 30 Hz)

2. **Windowing:** Each 3-second segment multiplied by cosine window:
   $$w(t) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{2}(1-\cos(4\pi t)) & 0 \le t \le \frac{1}{4} \\ 1 & \frac{1}{4} \le t \le L_s - \frac{1}{4} \\ \frac{1}{2}(1-\cos(4\pi t)) & L_s - \frac{1}{4} \le t \le L_s \end{cases}$$

3. **Normalization:** Divide by absolute maximum value in segment

4. **Binarization:** $b_i = 1$ if $|x_i| > V_0 = 0.2$, else $b_i = 0$

5. **TCSC parameter N:** Percentage of samples crossing threshold:
   $$N = \frac{\text{Number of samples crossing } V_0}{\text{Total samples}} \times 100$$

6. **Decision on 8-second episodes:** Average over 6 consecutive 3-second windows (1-second step):
   $$N_a = \frac{1}{L_e - 2} \sum_{i=1}^{L_e-2} N_i, \quad L_e = 8$$

   VF detected if $N_a > N_d = 48$ (optimized for high specificity; for high sensitivity use $25 < N_d < 35$)

## Data Analysis

- **MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database (MITDB):** 48 files, 2 channels, 1805 s each; sampled at 360 Hz
- **CU Database (CUDB):** 35 files, 1 channel, 508 s each
- **Episode labeling:** 8-second episodes; classified as VF if VF annotation > 4 s in episode
- **Continuous analysis:** 1-second step
- **Total decisions:** 2×48×(1805−7) + 35×(508−7) = **190,143 per algorithm**
- VT classified as "no VF"

## Conclusion

TCSC is simple (comparison + count), computationally efficient, and well-suited for real-time AED implementation. It outperforms all compared algorithms on the largest standardized dataset evaluation. Robust in presence of other pathologic cases included in databases.
