# Comparison of Four Techniques for Recognition of Ventricular Fibrillation from the Surface ECG

**Authors:** R. H. Clayton, A. Murray, R. W. F. Campbell
**Journal:** Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing, March 1993, 31:111–117
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02446668
**PMID:** 8331990

---

## Abstract

Four VF detection techniques assessed using CCU recordings. The VF-filter achieved 77% sensitivity; TCI, spectrum, and ACF had 53%, 46%, and 67% respectively. For specificity (VF-like data): TCI 93%, spectrum 72%, VF-filter 55%, ACF 38%. Overall (full recordings): TCI 93% sens/60% spec; spectrum 80%/60%; VF-filter 93%/20%; ACF 87%/0%.

---

## ECG Data

- Source: Freeman Hospital CCU, single bipolar chest lead, 250 Hz sampling
- A simple VF detector triggered recording (3–6 Hz RMS >75% of 2-s average)
- **VF recordings:** 15 × 40-second recordings (13 included onset transition)
- **VF-like recordings:** 10 × 40-second recordings (muscle artefact, atrial flutter, etc.)
- Test sets: 70 × 4-s VF segments (pure VF), 40 × 4-s VF-like segments
- Segment: 1024 data points = 4.096 s; 10 non-overlapping segments per 40-s recording

---

## Four Detection Techniques

### 1. Threshold Crossing Intervals (TCI) — Thakor et al. 1990

Each 4-s segment divided into four 1-s sub-segments. Third-order Butterworth HP (0.7 Hz) + 10th-order LP (50 Hz). Threshold = 20% of sub-segment maximum.

Sequential hypothesis testing (Thakor 1990):
$$F = \frac{1}{\sigma_{VF}^2}\sum(T_i - \mu_{VF})^2 - \frac{1}{\sigma_{VT}^2}\sum(T_i - \mu_{VT})^2$$

VT if $F \ge 2\log_e\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha} + 2m\log_e\frac{\sigma_{VT}}{\sigma_{VF}}$; VF if $F \le 2\log_e\frac{\beta}{1-\alpha} + 2m\log_e\frac{\sigma_{VT}}{\sigma_{VF}}$

Parameters used (from Ripley et al. 1989, intracardiac data):
- VF: μ = 158 ms, σ = 16 ms
- VT: μ = 350 ms, σ = 75 ms
- α = β = 0.01; TCI threshold > 400 ms → non-VF

**Note:** Original Thakor parameters (VF: 105 ms, VT: 220 ms) gave only 4% sensitivity on this dataset, demonstrating strong parameter dependence.

### 2. Autocorrelation Function (ACF) — Chen et al. 1987

VF is relatively aperiodic; SR is periodic/quasi-periodic. Seven highest peaks in short-term ACF identified; linear regression fitted to (peak order, lag):
$$y = a + bx$$

Variance ratio $b/[R/(m-2)]$ calculated. If > F-statistic for p=0.95 → linear relationship exists → not VF.

### 3. VF-Filter — Kuo and Dillman 1978

Based on VF approximating a sinusoidal waveform. Mean period T from FFT:
$$T = 2\pi \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|V_i|}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|V_i - V_{i-1}|}$$

VF-filter leakage (narrow band-stop filter centered at mean frequency):
$$\text{leakage} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|V_i + V_{i-T/2}|}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(|V_i| + |V_{i-T/2}|)}$$

VF identified if leakage < 0.625. (Only higher threshold used; no QRS detector.)

### 4. Spectrum Analysis — Barro et al. 1989

FFT with Hamming window. Four descriptors: FSMN, A1, A2, A3 (see SPEC-1989.md).

VF if: FSMN ≤ 1.55, A1 > 0.19, A2 ≥ 0.45, A3 ≤ 0.09.

## Key Findings

- All algorithms failed on at least one VF recording
- ACF identified VF in ALL VF-like recordings (specificity = 0%) — highly susceptible to irregular rhythms
- VF-filter most sensitive but susceptible to periodic artefacts (atrial flutter)
- Spectrum algorithm: poor sensitivity partly due to low-amplitude VF affecting A1 (mean A1=0.13 vs threshold 0.19)
- TCI: best overall, but highly dependent on correct parameterization (VF frequency assumption)
- Results suggest VF has wider property range than often realized
- Combining algorithms with a QRS detector would likely improve performance

## Algorithm Implementation Notes for VFLEAK-1978

The VF-filter leakage formula in Appendix:
$$\text{leakage} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m} |V_i + V_{i-T/2}|}{\sum_{i=1}^{m} (|V_i| + |V_{i-T/2}|)}$$

where T = number of data points per mean period.

In the original Kuo & Dillman (1978) system, two thresholds were used:
- Signal amplitude > 1/3 of last QRS amplitude: VF if leakage < 0.406
- Otherwise: VF if leakage < 0.625

In this study (no QRS detector): only threshold 0.625 used.
